# Telling farmers when to irrigate, crop by crop, field by field

**Company:** Njivomer Agro d.o.o. [C022], Agri-tech (farm software, sensing, drones), 61 employees, Sombor.  
**Researcher:** Nenad Ilić, Associate Professor, Faculty of Agriculture, Department of Field and Vegetable Crops [R0662].  
**Format:** Joint project  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Associate Professor (principal investigator) (academic) and Software project lead (part-time liaison) (company)  

### The problem
- Njivomer Agro cannot build the irrigation scheduling module its farm clients have requested because none of its staff can implement evapotranspiration modelling correctly. [C022-CH3]
- The company receives daily weather data and holds spatial soil data for most of western Vojvodina but lacks the calculation engine that converts those inputs into a daily water-balance estimate for a given crop and growth stage. [C022-CH3]
- The engine must be built and validated using real measured data from at least one irrigation season and delivered before the 2026 irrigation season. [C022-CH3]

### Work packages
1. How accurately does the FAO Penman-Monteith ET₀ engine reproduce daily soil water status on two monitored parcels, measured against continuous multi-depth probe readings collected over one full growing season?
2. What locally calibrated crop coefficients for at least two crops grown in western Vojvodina differ from FAO-56 tabulated values by growth stage, measured against seasonal soil water balance records from the same two monitored parcels?
3. Do the water-balance engine irrigation timing recommendations agree with prospective sign-off by company agronomists on at least fifty parcels observed during the field season, measured against those agronomists' independent timing decisions?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
