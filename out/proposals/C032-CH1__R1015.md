# Finding network threats on industrial control systems without labelled attack data

**Company:** SecureEdge Labs [C032], Cybersecurity, 19 employees, NTP Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Vuk Đukić, Associate Professor, Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Computing and Control Engineering [R1015].  
**Format:** Joint project  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Associate Professor, Department of Computing and Control Engineering (academic) and Security Engineering Lead (company)

### The problem
- The challenge is to detect unusual behaviour on Modbus and BACnet networks without labelled attack data. [C032-CH1]
- Hand-written rules miss slow, low-volume probing, and statistical baselines drift with shift patterns and seasonal production changes. [C032-CH1]
- Any detection model must run inference on the edge sensor, which has 512 MB of RAM and a modest ARM core. [C032-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Which unsupervised model architectures can detect anomalies in Modbus traffic when trained only on benign packet logs?
2. How can a detection model be adapted to run within the memory and compute limits of an ARM-class edge sensor?
3. How can the detection approach be extended to cover BACnet protocol traffic alongside Modbus?
4. What mechanism can allow a trained model to handle gradual drift in normal traffic patterns caused by shift and seasonal production changes?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 – Baseline model (M1–M3) | Train unsupervised anomaly detection models on anonymised Modbus packet logs from the three customer sites and evaluate reconstruction error against held-out benign traffic. | A baseline anomaly detection model evaluated on held-out benign Modbus traffic with reported detection and false-positive rates. |
| Phase 2 – Edge validation (M3–M6) | Adapt the best-performing model variant through quantisation or architecture pruning and validate inference performance on the sensor's ARM hardware against its memory and compute constraints. | An edge-feasible model variant confirmed to run within the sensor's hardware limits on the ARM test environment. |
| Phase 3 – BACnet extension and drift handling (M6–M9) | Extend the model to BACnet protocol traffic and design and test a federated or incremental learning mechanism to handle baseline drift without requiring labelled data. | A model covering both Modbus and BACnet traffic together with a validated drift-handling mechanism. |
| Phase 4 – Delivery (M9–M12) | Run end-to-end evaluation across all three site datasets, prepare integration documentation, and compile the final performance report. | Final models, integration documentation, and a performance report delivered to the company. |

### What each side brings
**Company** [C032]
- About eighteen months of raw packet logs from three customer sites, averaging around four gigabytes per day per site, are available in anonymised form. [C032-CH1]
- The EdgeWatch sensor captures all traffic on a customer's Modbus and BACnet segments and sends parsed records to the backend. [C032-CH1]
- The company maintains four security engineers who do the core research and product work and who will provide protocol expertise and integration support. [C032]
**University** [R1015]
- An unsupervised approach uses autoencoder-based neural networks trained exclusively on benign traffic to establish a baseline behavioural profile without requiring labelled attack data. [SYN-PUB-10523]
- Experimental results demonstrate detection rates exceeding 94% across all attack categories, with a false positive rate below 2% for Modbus traffic scenarios including unauthorised function code injection and coil manipulation. [SYN-PUB-10523]
- A federated learning framework for distributed intrusion detection enables each edge node to train a local anomaly detection model on its own traffic and share only model updates, which supports handling drift across distributed sites. [SYN-PUB-10526]

### Why this pair
- The challenge notes that public datasets for OT protocols are thin and often do not match what the company sees in the field, and that customers do not want attack simulations run on live plant, leaving the company without labelled training data. [C032-CH1]
- The researcher has published an unsupervised anomaly detection framework specifically designed for Modbus traffic analysis, and the company holds eighteen months of raw packet logs from three customer sites that provide the field-captured traffic the method requires for training and evaluation.
