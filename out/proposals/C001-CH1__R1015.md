# On-Device Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Modbus and BACnet Industrial Networks

**Company:** ArmaturaSec d.o.o. [C001], Cybersecurity, 67 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Faculty:** Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Computing and Control Engineering [R1015].  
**Format:** Joint project  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal Investigator (Associate Professor, milestone review and publication coordination) (academic) and ArmaturaSec Technical Lead Engineer (milestone sign-off and NDA compliance) (company)

### The problem
- ArmaturaSec has no labelled attack traffic from real deployments, making supervised classifiers ineffective for their ProtoSense module. [C001-CH1]
- A previous attempt using public ICS datasets for supervised classification produced excessive false positives, causing clients to disable alerts entirely. [C001-CH1]
- The required solution must learn site-specific normal behaviour from benign traffic alone and produce explanations suitable for non-specialist operational staff. [C001-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Can an autoencoder-based anomaly detection model trained solely on site-specific benign Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP traffic achieve reliable detection without labelled attack examples?
2. What traffic window sizes and feature encodings yield the best balance of detection rate and false-positive rate on resource-constrained embedded hardware?
3. How can reconstruction-error signals be translated into plain-language alerts interpretable by non-specialist plant operators?
4. Does a model trained on one site's traffic generalise sufficiently to a new site, or is per-site retraining always required?
