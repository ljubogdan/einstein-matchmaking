# Deployable Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Modbus and BACnet Edge Sensors

**Company:** SecureEdge Labs [C032], Cybersecurity, 19 employees, NTP Novi Sad.  
**Faculty:** Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Computing and Control Engineering [R1008].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Full Professor, Department of Computing and Control Engineering (academic) and Head of Engineering, SecureEdge Labs (company)

### The problem
- The company has almost no labelled examples of attacks on its networks, and public OT datasets do not match what is seen in the field. [C032-CH1]
- Hand-written rules miss slow probing, and statistical baselines drift with shift patterns and seasonal production changes, generating noise that overwhelms analysts. [C032-CH1]
- Any deployed model must fit within 512 MB of RAM on a modest ARM core, ruling out cloud-scale architectures. [C032-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Can unsupervised sequence models trained on unlabelled Modbus and BACnet traffic reliably detect slow, low-volume probing that rule-based detectors miss?
2. How should recalibration windows be sized and scheduled to track shift-pattern and seasonal drift without generating excessive false alerts?
3. Which combination of quantisation and structured pruning best preserves detection performance while meeting the inference latency and memory budget of the target edge sensor?
4. How does model performance on anonymised customer traffic from multiple sites compare with performance measured on controlled testbed captures?
