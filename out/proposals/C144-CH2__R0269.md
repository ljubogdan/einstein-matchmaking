# Keeping the ultrafiltration step running for a full 72-hour batch

**Company:** Ferment-Pro d.o.o. [C144], Biotech, diagnostics and lab services, 58 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Ivona Bogdanović, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Chemical Engineering [R0269].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal Investigator (project lead and reporting) (academic) and Head of Production (milestone sign-off and escalation point at M3, M6, M9, M12) (company)  

### The problem
- The ultrafiltration step runs at only 55% of nominal throughput because membranes foul within 36 hours, forcing two CIP cycles per batch instead of one. [C144-CH2]
- The extra cleaning cycle adds chemical cost, water, time, and roughly 11% to total production cost per batch of protease concentrate. [C144-CH2]
- Varying transmembrane pressure, crossflow velocity, and switching membrane suppliers has not resolved the fouling, and the depth-filter setup before the UF skid is not catching enough residual whole cells and cell debris. [C144-CH2]

### Work packages
1. What fouling mechanism — cake filtration, intermediate blocking, or pore constriction — governs flux decline in the 10 kDa PES step, measured against resistance-in-series and Hermia model fits on controlled bench-scale flux-time experiments using authentic feed samples?
2. Which pre-treatment modification reduces total fouling resistance sufficiently to sustain flux for 72 hours per batch, measured against water-flux recovery on bench-scale UF rigs using authentic process stream samples?
3. Does the modified pre-treatment or operating protocol achieve full 72-hour runs on the company UF skid at close to nominal flux, measured against pressure and flow logs from at least three consecutive validation batches?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |

| Phase 2 — Bench-scale pre-treatment and protocol development (months 4–6) | University team designs and runs bench-scale cross-flow UF trials on authentic process stream using university cross-flow UF rigs; company technologist prepares and ships feed samples; pre-treatment options and operating parameter changes are tested against flux-time performance and water-flux recovery. | Ranked shortlist of pre-treatment or protocol modifications with bench-scale flux and fouling resistance data, delivered at M6. |
| Phase 3 — Sensor installation and pilot-scale validation on company UF skid (months 7–9) | University team supplies portable inline permeate-side UV absorbance and conductivity monitoring instrumentation and installs it on the company UF skid before trials begin; company technologist implements selected pre-treatment and protocol modifications under university PI guidance; pressure and flow data logged for each run. | Pilot validation dataset from at least three consecutive UF runs on company skid with inline CIP endpoint data, delivered at M9. |
| Phase 4 — Protocol finalisation and reporting (months 10–12) | University team analyses pilot data, refines CIP schedule and pre-treatment protocol, and prepares final documentation; company technologist reviews draft for confidential content within 30-day review window. | Final report with optimised pre-treatment protocol, operating parameters, and CIP recommendations, delivered at M12. |
