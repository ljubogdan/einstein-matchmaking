# Reaching full 72-hour ultrafiltration runs without extra cleaning stops

**Company:** Ferment-Pro d.o.o. [C144], Biotech, diagnostics and lab services, 58 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Ivona Bogdanović, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Chemical Engineering [R0269].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal Investigator (project lead and reporting) (academic) and Process Development Technologist (part-time liaison and on-site coordination) (company)  

### The problem
- The ultrafiltration step runs at only 55% of nominal throughput because membranes foul with residual biomass within 36 hours, forcing two cleaning cycles per batch instead of one. [C144-CH2]
- The extra cleaning cycle adds chemical cost, water, time, and roughly 11% to total production cost per batch of protease concentrate. [C144-CH2]
- The company suspects residual whole cells and cell debris pass the pre-filtration stage, and has no membrane characterisation tools beyond visual inspection and water-flux recovery testing. [C144-CH2]

### Work packages
1. What foulant species and deposition mechanisms cause flux decline in the 10 kDa PES spiral-wound step, measured against AFM and resistance-in-series analysis of membrane samples from production batches?
2. What pre-filtration configuration reduces cell and debris loading to a level that sustains flux for 72 hours, measured against logged TMP and flux profiles from at least five bench-scale runs?
3. What CIP protocol composition and endpoint criteria restore pure water flux recovery to a level consistent with nominal flux after a scripted 72-hour production-representative run on the bench-scale rig, with a pass criterion tied to the nominal flux target?
4. Does the optimised pre-filtration and CIP combination achieve a 72-hour run at nominal flux on the production skid, measured against the membrane manufacturer's rated flux and the company's nominal 72-hour run specification?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Fouling characterisation (months 1–3) | University team analyses membrane samples and two years of batch logs using AFM, resistance-in-series modelling, and inline data review; company technologist provides sample access and data export. | Root-cause report identifying foulant species, deposition mechanism, and the pre-filtration gap, delivered at M3. |
| Phase 2 — Bench-scale protocol development (months 4–6) | University team runs scripted 72-hour fouling and cleaning trials on the cross-flow UF rig and bench-scale CIP test station using authentic protease broth supplied by the company; pass criterion for each protocol is pure water flux recovery consistent with the nominal flux target after each scripted run. | Shortlist of two or three pre-filtration and CIP protocol combinations with bench-scale flux and recovery data, delivered at M6. |
| Phase 3 — Production skid validation (months 7–9) | Company technologist and university research staff run the preferred protocol on the production UF skid; university team monitors TMP, flux, and CIP endpoint signals using inline instrumentation brought on-site; performance is assessed against the membrane manufacturer's rated flux and the company's nominal 72-hour run specification. | Validated protocol achieving a 72-hour run at nominal flux on the production skid, with recorded TMP and flux profiles, delivered at M9. |
| Phase 4 — Final reporting (months 10–12) | University team compiles all characterisation data, bench results, and skid trial outcomes; company technologist reviews for confidential content within 30 days under publication agreement. | Final report with optimised pre-filtration and CIP protocol, implementation recommendations, and a dataset the company holds as background IP, delivered at M12. |
