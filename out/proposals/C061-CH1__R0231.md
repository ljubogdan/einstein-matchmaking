# Finding why pumpkin seed oil goes rancid and fixing it

**Company:** Zlatna Njiva d.o.o. [C061], Food production and food tech, 87 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Radomir Mirković, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry [R0231].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal investigator, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry (academic) and Quality/production manager (company)  

### The problem
- Cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil hits a peroxide value above the acceptable threshold between three and five months after bottling, while competitors achieve 9–12 months on shelf. [C061-CH1]
- The company spends roughly 180,000 RSD per year on returned and written-off stock from this issue and has not identified the dominant oxidation pathway. [C061-CH1]
- The nitrogen-flush equipment is a single nozzle above the fill point, leaving residual headspace oxygen levels unmeasured and the company uncertain how much protection it provides. [C061-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Which oxidation pathway — autoxidation, photo-oxidation, trace-metal-catalysed, or enzymatic — produces the fastest rise in peroxide value in pumpkin seed oil across batches, measured against multi-marker panel results at months 2, 5, and 8?
2. Do batches with higher tocopherol content show longer Rancimat induction periods, measured against HPLC-quantified tocopherol content and Rancimat results for the same lots across all sampled batches?
3. Does reducing headspace residual oxygen at the fill point change the multi-marker oxidation trajectory, measured against PV and p-anisidine value in paired bottles filled under current and modified flush conditions over the storage period?
4. Does the modified bottling procedure produce a longer Rancimat induction period than the current procedure, measured against Rancimat results from trial bottles filled at the end of Phase 2 and analysed at month 10?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Baseline characterisation (months 1–2) | University lab runs GC-FID fatty acid profiling and HPLC-FLD tocopherol quantification on freshly pressed oil from three batches; company lab technician prepares and ships samples and conducts parallel peroxide value titrations on the same lots. | Fatty acid profile and tocopherol baseline dataset for three batches; written baseline oxidation status summary delivered to company at M2. |
| Phase 2 — Multi-marker panel, headspace measurement, and trial fills (months 3–5) | University measures PV, p-anisidine value, TBARS, and Rancimat induction period on stored samples at months 3 and 5; university rents a portable oxygen analyser, covering rental cost under analytical consumables, and measures headspace residual oxygen on the company's bottling line; at month 5 the company lab technician fills trial bottles under two flush conditions — current setting and adjusted nozzle duration — for Phase 4 validation. | Multi-marker oxidation panel dataset, headspace oxygen measurement report, and trial bottle sets sealed and logged at M5; milestone M5 report delivered to company. |
| Phase 3 — Pathway identification and process recommendations (months 6–8) | University interprets combined marker patterns across all batches and time points to identify dominant oxidation pathway(s); university drafts process recommendations adjustable on the existing bottling line; company lab technician conducts routine PV checks on stored trial bottles from Phase 2. | Identification of dominant oxidation pathway and draft process recommendations with supporting data; M8 interim report delivered to company. |
| Phase 4 — Rancimat validation and final report (months 9–10) | University runs Rancimat induction period measurement and full multi-marker analysis on trial bottles from Phase 2, which have now accumulated at least five months of storage; company lab technician provides parallel PV titration results on the same bottles; university drafts final report. | Final report with Rancimat-validated process changes, a decision framework for routine monitoring, and all raw datasets delivered to company at M10. |

### Why this pair
- The company does not have gas chromatography equipment or specialist oxidation training in-house, while the research group specialises in lipid oxidation mechanisms in cold-pressed seed oils and holds the instruments the analysis requires. [C061-CH1] [R0231]
- Production batches vary in their oxidation profile, and the company operates a cold-press production line with nitrogen flushing. [C061-CH1] [C061]
- Research on headspace oxygen in cold-pressed seed oils shows that nitrogen flushing significantly reduced peroxide value and that the interaction between light and headspace atmosphere was statistically significant, indicating that light accelerates oxidation most strongly when oxygen is available.
