# Finding and fixing what causes cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil to go rancid early

**Company:** Zlatna Njiva d.o.o. [C061], Food production and food tech, 87 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Radomir Mirković, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry [R0231].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Full Professor, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry (academic) and Quality and Production Manager (company)

### The problem
- The cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil goes rancid within 4 months on shelf while competitors manage 9–12 months, and the company loses about 15% of each batch to returns. [C061-CH1]
- Despite nitrogen flushing and dark glass bottling, peroxide value rises above 15 by month four, and the company is not sure how much residual oxygen remains in the headspace. [C061-CH1]
- The company has no gas chromatography equipment and has never done a fatty acid profile breakdown or a tocopherol content analysis in-house, leaving the dominant oxidation pathway unidentified. [C061-CH1]

### Work packages
1. What is the dominant oxidation pathway in the cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil: autoxidation, photo-oxidative, enzymatic, or trace-metal mediated?
2. What level of residual oxygen remains in the headspace under the current nitrogen-flush protocol, and does it exceed the threshold at which oxidation accelerates?
3. How do fatty acid composition and tocopherol content vary across production batches, and do these variations correlate with differences in observed oxidation rate?
4. Which process changes, requiring no major capital equipment purchase, would most reduce the rate of peroxide accumulation and extend shelf life?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 – Baseline characterisation (Months 1–2) | The research laboratory measures fatty acid profile by GC-FID, tocopherol content by HPLC, and initial peroxide formation kinetics on samples drawn from multiple production batches. The company's lab technician prepares samples and conducts parallel iodometric peroxide checks. | Baseline analytical report covering fatty acid composition, tocopherol levels, and initial oxidation kinetics, delivered by Month 2. |
| Phase 2 – Headspace oxygen measurement (Months 3–5) | Residual oxygen in the headspace is measured on the company's nitrogen-flush bottling line under current operating conditions and under adjusted flush parameters to assess whether the current set-up meets accepted oxygen thresholds for cold-pressed oil packaging. | Headspace oxygen measurement report and assessment of current nitrogen-flush effectiveness, delivered by Month 5. |
| Phase 3 – Oxidation pathway identification (Months 6–8) | The full oxidation marker panel—peroxide value, p-anisidine value, TBARS, and Rancimat induction period—is applied to aged oil samples from multiple batches and results are cross-referenced with the baseline fatty acid and tocopherol data to rank contributing oxidation pathways. | Oxidation pathway identification report with ranked contributing factors, delivered by Month 8. |
| Phase 4 – Process recommendations and quality control protocol (Months 9–12) | Ranked pathway findings and headspace oxygen results are translated into implementable process changes requiring no major capital equipment purchase, and a quality control protocol for ongoing shelf-life monitoring is developed and validated against the company's routine measurement capabilities. | Final report with ranked process recommendations and quality control protocol, delivered by Month 12. |

**University** [R0231]
- Published kinetic work on cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil records rate constants from 0.018 to 0.091 meq O₂/(kg·day) across storage temperatures, with activation energy of 48.3 kJ/mol consistent with autoxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids rather than photosensitised oxidation. [SYN-PUB-02918]
