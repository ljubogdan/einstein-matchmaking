# Extended Shelf Life for Cold-Pressed Pumpkin Seed Oil Through Oxidation Pathway Control

**Company:** Zlatna Njiva d.o.o. [C061], Food production and food tech, 87 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Faculty:** Faculty of Technology, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry [R0231].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal investigator as scientific lead and single point of contact (academic) and Quality responsible (lab technician) as day-to-day liaison; company director as decision authority (company)

### The problem
- The oil consistently exceeds acceptable peroxide thresholds between three and five months after bottling, far short of the nine-to-twelve months competitors achieve. [C061-CH1]
- The current nitrogen flush relies on a single nozzle above the fill point, leaving actual residual headspace oxygen unknown and potentially well above safe levels. [C061-CH1]
- Without fatty acid profiling, tocopherol analysis, or gas chromatography, the company cannot distinguish whether autoxidation, photo-oxidation, or trace-metal catalysis is the dominant degradation route. [C061-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Which oxidation pathway — autoxidative, photo-oxidative, or metal-catalysed — dominates peroxide accumulation in this company's cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil?
2. What is the actual residual oxygen concentration in the headspace after the current single-nozzle nitrogen flush, and how does it correlate with oxidation rate across batches?
3. Does the tocopherol content in fresh oil vary between batches, and does this variation predict differences in oxidative stability?
4. Which combination of low-capital process adjustments — filling-line modifications, antioxidant additions, or storage conditions — offers the greatest improvement in shelf life?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 – Baseline Analytical Panel | Collect fresh and aged oil samples across at least three batches; run peroxide value, p-anisidine value, TBARS, GC-FID fatty acid profile, HPLC-FLD tocopherol quantification, and Rancimat induction period on each sample. | Quantified baseline oxidation status and natural antioxidant inventory for each batch, enabling inter-batch comparison. |
| Month 2–3 – Pathway Identification and Headspace Audit | Apply kinetic modelling to peroxide accumulation data; measure residual headspace oxygen on the active filling line using appropriate instrumentation; assess chlorophyll and trace-metal contribution through UV/Vis spectrophotometry. | Written pathway identification report ranking autoxidative, photo-oxidative, and metal-catalysed mechanisms by their relative contribution, plus a headspace oxygen audit result compared against the recommended 2% threshold. |
| Month 4–5 – Intervention Testing | Formulate and test a small set of low-capital interventions — adjusted flushing protocol, antioxidant type or dose, or temperature modification — using Rancimat and accelerated storage trials on company-supplied samples. | Ranked effectiveness data for each tested intervention, supporting prioritisation without capital expenditure. |
| Month 6 – Final Report and Recommendations | Integrate all analytical and intervention data into a final written report with prioritised, step-by-step process change recommendations that can be implemented on existing equipment. | Delivered final report with actionable, ranked process changes and supporting data package for regulatory or certification use. |
