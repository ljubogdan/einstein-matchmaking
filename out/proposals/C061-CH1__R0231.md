# Extended Shelf Life for Cold-Pressed Pumpkin Seed Oil Through Oxidation Pathway Control

**Company:** Zlatna Njiva d.o.o. [C061], Food production and food tech, 87 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Faculty:** Faculty of Technology, Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry [R0231].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal Investigator (Full Professor): technical delivery, milestone reporting, single point of contact for all findings (academic) and Quality/lab responsible (Vesna Marković role): sample logistics, routine measurements, milestone sign-off (company)

### The problem
- The pumpkin seed oil exceeds acceptable peroxide value thresholds between three and five months after bottling. [C061-CH1]
- The current nitrogen-flush setup uses a single nozzle above the fill point, leaving the actual residual headspace oxygen level unknown and potentially above safe limits. [C061-CH1]
- The company does not have gas chromatography, fatty acid profiling, or tocopherol analysis capabilities. [C061-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Which oxidation pathway — autoxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids, photosensitised oxidation, or trace-metal-catalysed oxidation — is the primary driver of peroxide accumulation in this oil?
2. What is the actual residual oxygen concentration in the headspace after the current nitrogen flush, and how does it compare to recommended thresholds for small-scale cold-press operations?
3. How do fatty acid composition and tocopherol content vary across batches, and how do these differences correlate with observed differences in oxidative stability?
4. Which combination of low-capital process adjustments to flushing, filling, storage temperature, or antioxidant addition would realistically extend shelf life to nine-to-twelve months?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Baseline Audit (M1–M3) | Measure residual headspace oxygen on the filling line; collect multi-batch oil samples at bottling and at monthly intervals; run peroxide value, p-anisidine value, TBARS, GC-FID fatty acid profile, HPLC-FLD tocopherol content, and Rancimat induction period on fresh samples. | Quantified headspace oxygen level and a complete baseline oxidation marker panel across batches, establishing the starting point for kinetic modelling. |
| Phase 2 — Pathway Identification (M4–M6) | Fit pseudo-first-order kinetic models to time-series peroxide data; interpret activation energy and marker ratios to distinguish autoxidation from photo-oxidative and metal-catalysed routes; correlate pathway signatures with fatty acid and tocopherol profiles. | Written oxidation pathway identification report naming the dominant mechanism and ranking contributing factors by their measured impact. |
| Phase 3 — Process Intervention and Pilot Validation (M7–M9) | Design a ranked set of implementable process changes covering headspace oxygen reduction, storage temperature, and antioxidant strategy; prepare pilot batches applying selected changes; monitor oxidation markers monthly against the baseline. | Validated pilot-batch data confirming which process changes produce measurable improvement in oxidative stability without requiring major capital equipment. |
| Phase 4 — Final Reporting and Protocol Handover (M10–M12) | Consolidate kinetic data into a shelf-life projection model; document all recommended protocols in a format the in-house technician can apply for routine production; prepare the final report for company review and sign-off. | Final report with shelf-life projections, ranked process-change protocol, and quality-check procedures ready for immediate production use. |
