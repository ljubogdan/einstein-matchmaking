# Keeping MBR membranes running longer between cleans in dairy plants

**Company:** ReVita Water [C088], Environmental engineering, 27 employees, NTP Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Aleksandar Marić, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Chemical Engineering [R0745].  
**Format:** Joint project  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Full Professor as principal investigator and technical lead (academic) and ReVita process engineer as project liaison and site coordinator (company)  

### The problem
- Operators at two dairy plant sites are forced to run a full CIP every five to seven days, against a scheduled interval of two weeks. [C088-CH2]
- Each full CIP uses roughly 200 litres of citric acid and caustic solution per unit and takes the system offline for four to six hours. [C088-CH2]
- The company does not have the tools or know-how to characterise the foulant properly. [C088-CH2]

### Work packages
1. What is the chemical and physical composition of the gel-layer foulant on MBR membranes at the two dairy sites, measured against SEM and ATR-FTIR reference characterisation of sampled membranes?
2. Does the foulant composition shift with season in a pattern that matches three years of service records and on-site temperature logs?
3. Which combination of pre-treatment screening, aeration intensity, and sludge retention time reduces bench-scale fouling rate, measured against flux decline and fouling resistance on a test rig using site-sourced dairy effluent?
4. Does the best-performing bench-scale intervention extend the cleaning interval to at least twelve days at the Temerin or Bečej pilot site, measured against transmembrane pressure logs over a six-week trial?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Foulant characterisation (months 1–3) | University team collects membrane coupons and effluent samples at both dairy sites with support from the company lab technician; analyses foulant composition by SEM, ATR-FTIR, and hydraulic resistance methods using university analytical equipment. | Foulant characterisation report: composition, resistance-in-series breakdown, and seasonal profile mapped against three years of company service records. |
| Phase 2 — Bench-scale intervention trials (months 4–9) | University team designs and runs bench-scale membrane flux trials using dairy effluent from the two sites; tests pre-screening mesh sizes, aeration intensity levels, and sludge retention time variations; company lab technician measures COD and TSS at each condition. | Bench-scale dataset and ranked intervention shortlist, with flux decline and fouling resistance values for each tested condition. |
| Phase 3 — Pilot trial (months 10–15) | Company process engineer implements the top-ranked intervention at the Temerin or Bečej site; university team monitors transmembrane pressure and collects membrane samples at intervals; scripted CIP events log chemical volume and downtime. | Six-week pilot log with transmembrane pressure curves, cleaning interval data, and CIP chemical consumption per event, confirming whether twelve-day intervals are reached. |
| Phase 4 — Recommendations and reporting (months 16–18) | University team drafts final report and a peer-reviewed paper covering fouling mechanisms and intervention outcomes; company process engineer reviews for confidential content within 60 days under the agreed publication review window. | Final practical recommendations report delivered to the company and a manuscript submitted for publication. |
