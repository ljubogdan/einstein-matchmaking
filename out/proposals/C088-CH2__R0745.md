# Keeping dairy MBR membranes clean longer between shutdowns

**Company:** ReVita Water [C088], Environmental engineering, 27 employees, NTP Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Aleksandar Marić, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Chemical Engineering [R0745].  
**Format:** Joint project  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Full Professor as principal investigator (academic) and ReVita process engineer as project manager and day-to-day liaison (company)  

### The problem
- MBR membranes at two dairy plants are fouling so fast that operators must run a full CIP every five to seven days instead of the designed two-week interval. [C088-CH2]
- Each full CIP uses roughly 200 litres of citric acid and caustic solution per unit and takes the system offline for four to six hours. [C088-CH2]
- The company does not have the tools or know-how to characterise the foulant properly and suspects a combination of fats, proteins, and sugars is forming a gel layer. [C088-CH2]

### Work packages
1. What foulant compounds — fats, proteins, or sugars — dominate hydraulic resistance in the two dairy MBR installations, measured against ATR-FTIR and resistance-in-series analysis of membrane coupons from both sites?
2. Does a feed pre-screening or aeration adjustment reduce the rate of transmembrane pressure rise, measured against three months of logged TMP data from each site before and after the change?
3. Does the modified cleaning protocol extend the cleaning interval to at least twelve days, measured against two or more consecutive cleaning cycles logged on-site after the protocol change is applied?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Sampling and foulant characterisation (months 1–3) | University team collects membrane coupons, effluent samples, and seasonal wastewater samples from both sites with company field crew; applies ATR-FTIR, SEM, and resistance-in-series analysis in university laboratory. | Foulant characterisation report identifying dominant compounds and a baseline seasonal sample set from both sites (M3 milestone). |
| Phase 2 — Root-cause and seasonal analysis (months 4–9) | University correlates foulant data with company's three-year service records and temperature logs; company lab technician runs COD and TSS bench analytics on the seasonal samples collected in Phase 1. | Root-cause analysis report with seasonal pattern assessment and ranked list of contributing variables (M9 milestone). |
| Phase 3 — Protocol development and bench trials (months 10–14) | University designs pre-treatment and aeration modifications based on published fouling models; company process engineer tests candidates on the NTP Novi Sad pilot bench using scripted fat-and-protein feed mixtures. | Written recommendations on pre-treatment, aeration, and membrane selection for both dairy sites (M14 milestone). |
| Phase 4 — On-site validation (months 15–18) | Company installs recommended changes at both dairy plants; university monitors TMP logs and cleaning frequency; company lab technician records chemical consumption per CIP event across each cleaning cycle. | Confirmation of two or more consecutive cleaning cycles reaching twelve days, documented in a final joint technical report (M18 milestone). |
