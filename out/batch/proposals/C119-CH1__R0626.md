# Automatic artwork placement into packaging dielines

**Company:** Vizura Studio d.o.o. [C119], Marketing tech, media and creative studios, 14 employees, Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Ildikó Papp, Associate Professor, Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Graphic Engineering and Design [R0626].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal investigator (academic) and Project coordinator (company)  

### The problem
- The studio produces roughly 600 packaging variants a year and the bottleneck is fitting finished artwork into structural dielines for each SKU. [C119-CH1]
- On a shaped carton or a multi-panel sleeve the manual layout step can take two full days, making average turnaround five days from approved artwork to print-ready file. [C119-CH1]
- Existing Illustrator scripts handle only repetitive placement steps and one evaluated commercial plug-in assumed flat rectangular formats only, leaving shaped and multi-panel dielines unresolved. [C119-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Can a panel-detection algorithm correctly identify and bound all printable panels across rectangular, shaped, and multi-panel dieline types, measured against senior designer consensus on the representative dieline set supplied in Phase 1 and the 20 annotated SKU pairs supplied in Phase 2?
2. Does the auto-placement engine scale and position artwork within detected panels to within tolerance accepted by offset and digital print vendors, measured against vendor preflight pass rate on 20 test SKUs?
3. Does the end-to-end tool reduce artwork-to-print-ready turnaround on shaped and multi-panel cartons to two days or fewer, measured against current five-day baseline on a set of 20 representative SKUs?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 – Dieline parser prototype (months 1–3) | Research group designs geometric parsing algorithm using parametric dieline methods from background IP; senior designer supplies a representative set of rectangular and simple shaped dieline files with annotated panel boundaries. | A parser prototype that identifies panels, score lines, and bleed zones in rectangular and simple shaped cartons, delivered as a documented software module. |
| Phase 2 – Panel detection and auto-placement engine (months 4–6) | Research group implements and iterates panel-detection and artwork placement algorithms; senior designer provides 20 Vizura SKU artwork and dieline pairs as test cases and reviews placement outputs against production standards. | An auto-placement engine validated on 20 Vizura SKU samples, with a written validation report comparing output to senior designer consensus on those 20 annotated pairs. |
| Phase 3 – Vendor-ready output integration (months 7–9) | Research assistant integrates Illustrator and Esko ArtPro export paths; project coordinator coordinates acceptance testing with offset and digital print vendors using scripted fault cases on a test bench the company provides. | A confirmed vendor-ready output module with a preflight pass-rate report from at least one offset and one digital print vendor. |
| Phase 4 – Final tool and report (months 10–12) | Research group consolidates modules into a deployable tool; project coordinator runs end-to-end timing trials on 20 representative SKUs and documents results; research group authors technical report. | A working deployment-ready tool, a turnaround-time measurement report against the five-day baseline, and a technical report delivered to the company. |
