# A compostable lidding film that seals to existing rPET punnet lines

**Company:** FreshPak Solutions [C036], Packaging, 60 employees, NTP Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Stefan Đorđević, Associate Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Materials Engineering [R0274].  
**Format:** Joint project  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Associate Professor, Department of Materials Engineering (academic) and FreshPak R&D Manager (company)  

### The problem
- Several large German and Dutch retail chains will not accept conventional plastic lidding films from 2028 onward, creating a commercial deadline for two of the company's three biggest customers. [C036-CH2]
- Bench tests with two commercially available PLA-based films showed seal strength about thirty percent below the current spec, and one film showed visible clouding at 4 °C refrigerated storage. [C036-CH2]
- The company does not have the materials chemistry background to reformulate or identify better candidate polymers and needs a film certified to EN 13432 by mid-2027. [C036-CH2]

### Work packages
1. Which PLA-based seal-layer compositions achieve the company's seal-strength specification when bonded to rPET at existing temperatures and dwell times, measured against pull-test results from the company's EN 15507 bench?
2. How do wax or nanoclay modifications to PLA outer layers affect oxygen transmission rate and anti-fog performance, measured against the company's current lidding-film OTR and clarity specifications?
3. Do the optimised multilayer formulations pass EN 13432 disintegration and ecotoxicity criteria within the 12-week test period, measured against the standard's pass thresholds?
4. Which candidate formulation from the shortlist best satisfies all four criteria simultaneously, measured against the company's seal, OTR, anti-fog and compostability screen results?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Candidate shortlist (months 1–6) | University surveys PLA-based seal-layer chemistries including wax-modified and nanoclay-modified variants; company provides rPET punnet specifications and runs preliminary seal and OTR tests on supplied film samples. | A shortlist of candidate polymer formulations with preliminary seal-strength and OTR data, delivered as a written report at M6. |
| Phase 2 — Formulation optimisation (months 7–12) | University compounds and coextrudes multilayer film samples using compatibiliser approaches from prior work; company runs seal-strength pull tests to EN 15507 and anti-fog assessment on each variant at 4 °C. | An optimised formulation passing the company's seal-strength and anti-fog criteria, documented in a test report at M12. |
| Phase 3 — Compostability screening (months 13–18) | Company runs in-house disintegration screening against EN 13432 parameters on optimised film specimens; university conducts ecotoxicity and respirometric mineralisation tests at its faculty lab, using specimens prepared by the university. | A screening report combining company disintegration results and university ecotoxicity and respirometric data, confirming EN 13432 pass or identifying remaining gaps, delivered at M18. |
| Phase 4 — Certification data package (months 19–24) | University consolidates formulation data and characterisation results; company compiles seal, OTR, anti-fog and disintegration test records into a submission-ready dossier together with the university's ecotoxicity data. | A full data package structured for EN 13432 certification submission, ready by M24 and targeting submission by mid-2027. |
