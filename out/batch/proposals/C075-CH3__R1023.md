# Cutting exploratory pits by mapping legacy cable positions before breaking ground

**Company:** Voltmreža a.d. [C075], Energy, solar and grid services, 187 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Milena Nedić, Senior Research Associate, BioSense Institute, Remote Sensing and GIS Group [R1023].  
**Format:** Joint project  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Senior Research Associate, Remote Sensing and GIS Group (academic) and Engineering Office Lead (part-time project coordinator) (company)

### The problem
- Spatial offsets of two to four metres are common in the legacy drawings, and in some streets cables do not appear on any drawing at all. [C075-CH3]
- Before every excavation the civil crew spends between half a day and two full days digging exploratory pits. [C075-CH3]
- Two cable strikes last year cost a combined 340,000 dinars in repairs and project delays. [C075-CH3]

### Work packages
1. Can GPR hyperbola detection and multi-class classification reliably identify and distinguish cables from pipes in the legacy network streets within the service territory?
2. What credible interval widths on cable depth and lateral position can Monte Carlo dropout uncertainty quantification achieve on field data collected from legacy cable streets?
3. How does incorporating legacy GIS drawing offsets alongside GPR detections affect positional confidence interval estimates for cable location?
4. By how much does a probabilistic cable position map reduce the number of exploratory pits required per excavation site compared with current practice?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Baseline Survey (Months 1–3) | Conduct GPR surveys on three representative legacy cable streets; collect ground-truth cable positions from excavation pits using GPS positioning; compile legacy GIS drawing datasets and record offset distributions against ground truth. | Annotated baseline dataset with verified cable positions and offset measurements from three streets, forming training and evaluation data for algorithm development. |
| Phase 2 — Algorithm Development (Months 4–9) | Train hyperbola detection and multi-class cable/pipe classification models on the Phase 1 dataset; integrate Monte Carlo dropout for uncertainty quantification; build a prototype probabilistic mapping tool that outputs confidence intervals on cable depth and lateral position. | Prototype tool delivering per-detection position confidence intervals, assessed against Phase 1 ground truth, addressing the first two research questions. |
| Phase 3 — Field Validation (Months 10–15) | Deploy the prototype across a minimum of ten excavation sites in the service territory; record predicted cable positions and confidence interval coverage against excavation ground truth; measure change in number of exploratory pits required; assess the effect of legacy GIS offsets on confidence interval estimates. | Validated performance metrics across at least ten sites, including measured reduction in exploratory pit count and confidence interval coverage, addressing research questions three and four. |
| Phase 4 — Workflow Delivery (Months 16–18) | Integrate the validated algorithm into QGIS-compatible output layers; prepare site-engineer workflow documentation; deliver the final probabilistic cable mapping tool for operational use before excavation. | Final QGIS-compatible probabilistic cable mapping workflow and documentation delivered to the company for use by site engineers. |
