# Knowing where legacy cables are before the excavator moves

**Company:** Voltmreža a.d. [C075], Energy, solar and grid services, 187 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Milena Nedić, Senior Research Associate, BioSense Institute, Remote Sensing and GIS Group [R1023].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Senior Research Associate, BioSense Institute Remote Sensing and GIS Group (academic) and Lead civil/network engineer, Voltmreža engineering office (company)  

### The problem
- Underground cables installed between 1965 and 1990 appear on drawings with offsets of two to four metres or do not appear on any drawing at all. [C075-CH3]
- Before every excavation the civil crew spends between half a day and two full days digging exploratory pits, and two cable strikes last year cost a combined 340,000 dinars in repairs and project delays. [C075-CH3]
- The company seeks a detection or probabilistic mapping approach that gives site engineers a confidence interval on cable position before breaking ground. [C075-CH3]

### Work packages
1. Can GPR B-scan hyperbola detection reliably identify legacy cables in multi-cable corridors, measured against manual picks by three surveyors on 500 B-scans collected from the company's service territory?
2. What confidence-interval width does a Monte Carlo dropout depth model achieve on legacy cable targets, measured against excavation-verified ground-truth depths across at least 40 pits?
3. Does classifying detected hyperbolas as cables versus pipes improve site-engineer confidence in pre-excavation decisions, measured against per-class precision and recall on the Phase 1–2 dataset and a structured assessment by the engineering office during the Phase 4 trial?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — GPR survey (months 1–3) | BioSense researcher conducts GPR surveys with MALA ProEx across legacy cable areas selected by the Voltmreža engineering office; RD8100 readings collected in parallel at each site. | Annotated B-scan dataset covering representative legacy cable corridors, delivered to both parties. |
| Phase 2 — Algorithm prototype (months 4–6) | BioSense researcher applies hyperbola detection, clutter suppression, and CNN-based cable/pipe classification to the collected dataset; Voltmreža engineering office provides label review two days per month. | Detection and classification algorithm prototype with per-class precision and recall report. |
| Phase 3 — Probabilistic positioning tool (months 7–9) | BioSense researcher integrates Monte Carlo dropout depth estimation into the prototype; Voltmreža opens 40 verification pits at scripted locations to collect ground-truth depths for validation. | Probabilistic positioning tool producing 90% credible intervals on cable location, validated against excavation-verified depths from the 40 pits. |
| Phase 4 — Integration and final delivery (months 10–12) | BioSense researcher delivers QGIS-compatible output layer; Voltmreža engineering office runs the tool on three live excavation jobs and records a structured assessment of classification usefulness. | Deployable QGIS-compatible positioning layer, structured engineer assessment report, and final technical report delivered to Voltmreža. |
