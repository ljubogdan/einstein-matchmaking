# Keeping the ultrafiltration step running for a full batch without a mid-cycle clean

**Company:** Ferment-Pro d.o.o. [C144], Biotech, diagnostics and lab services, 58 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Ivona Bogdanović, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Chemical Engineering [R0269].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal Investigator (project lead and reporting) (academic) and Process Development Technologist (part-time liaison and on-site coordination) (company)  

### The problem
- The ultrafiltration step runs at only 55% of nominal throughput because membranes foul within 36 hours, forcing two cleaning cycles per batch instead of one. [C144-CH2]
- Each extra CIP cycle adds chemical cost, water, time, and roughly 11% to total production cost per batch of protease concentrate. [C144-CH2]
- The company suspects residual whole cells and cell debris pass the pre-filtration stage, and it has no membrane characterisation tools beyond visual inspection and water-flux recovery testing. [C144-CH2]

### Work packages
1. What foulant species — cells, cell debris, or protein aggregates — dominate resistance accumulation in the 10 kDa PES membranes, measured against AFM and resistance-in-series analysis of membrane samples from three consecutive production batches?
2. Does modifying the pre-filtration stage or operating protocol reduce fouling resistance sufficiently to sustain flux at nominal level for 72 hours, measured against bench-scale runs using authentic production broth?
3. Which CIP endpoint criterion — time-based or inline conductivity and UV absorbance — detects removal of authentic protease broth foulant more accurately, measured against water flux recovery on bench membranes fouled with authentic broth to a defined resistance level before each cleaning trial?
