# Eliminating the extra cleaning cycle in protease ultrafiltration

**Company:** Ferment-Pro d.o.o. [C144], Biotech, diagnostics and lab services, 58 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Ivona Bogdanović, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Chemical Engineering [R0269].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Full Professor (principal investigator and primary technical contact) (academic) and Process Development Technologist (part-time liaison and in-plant trial coordinator) (company)

### The problem
- The ultrafiltration step is supposed to run for about 72 hours per batch at rated flux, but membranes foul within 36 hours, requiring two cleaning cycles per batch instead of one. [C144-CH2]
- That extra cleaning cycle adds roughly 11% to total production cost per batch of protease concentrate. [C144-CH2]
- The company has no membrane characterisation tools beyond visual inspection and water-flux recovery testing, and suspects residual whole cells and cell debris passing the pre-filtration stage are the cause. [C144-CH2]

### Work packages
1. What foulant species and deposition mechanisms are responsible for flux decline in the production ultrafiltration membranes when processing protease fermentation broth?
2. Which depth-filtration configurations reduce residual cell and cell debris loading on the UF feed to levels that support the target run duration?
3. What CIP protocol parameters achieve reliable flux recovery after each cleaning cycle without accelerating membrane degradation?
4. How do optimised bench-scale pre-filtration and CIP protocols translate to operating parameters for the production-scale ultrafiltration skid?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1: Foulant characterisation (months 1–3) | Production process fluids and membrane samples are analysed using cross-flow ultrafiltration rigs, atomic force microscopy, and resistance-in-series methods to identify the primary foulant species and their deposition behaviour on the production membranes. | A fouling characterisation report identifying the primary foulant species and the mechanisms governing flux decline in the production process stream. |
| Phase 2: Pre-filtration and CIP candidate development (months 4–6) | Candidate depth-filtration configurations and CIP protocol parameters are developed and tested at bench scale using production-representative process streams, with flux recovery and fouling resistance as performance criteria. | A ranked shortlist of pre-filtration and CIP protocol candidates with supporting bench-scale flux and fouling resistance data. |
| Phase 3: Bench-scale validation (months 7–9) | The top-ranked pre-filtration and CIP combination is validated in extended bench-scale runs covering multiple simulated batch cycles to confirm sustained flux performance and cleaning efficacy across the target run duration. | A validated bench-scale protocol with flux and fouling resistance data demonstrating consistent performance over the target run duration. |
| Phase 4: Scale-up guidance and final report (months 10–12) | Validated protocols are translated into implementation-ready operating parameters for the production-scale ultrafiltration skid, with scale-up considerations and equipment modification options documented. | A final report delivering implementation-ready operating parameters, scale-up guidance, and recommendations for the production-scale ultrafiltration process. |
