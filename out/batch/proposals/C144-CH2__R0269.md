# Reaching full 72-hour ultrafiltration runs in protease production

**Company:** Ferment-Pro d.o.o. [C144], Biotech, diagnostics and lab services, 58 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Ivona Bogdanović, Full Professor, Faculty of Technology, Department of Chemical Engineering [R0269].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Principal Investigator, Department of Chemical Engineering (academic) and Process Development Technologist (company)  

### The problem
- The ultrafiltration step runs at only 55% of nominal throughput because membranes foul with residual biomass within 36 hours, forcing two CIP cycles per batch instead of one. [C144-CH2]
- The extra cleaning cycle adds chemical cost, water, time, and roughly 11% to total production cost per batch of protease concentrate. [C144-CH2]
- Varying transmembrane pressure, crossflow velocity, and switching membrane suppliers have all been tried without improvement, and the company has no membrane characterisation tools beyond visual inspection and water-flux recovery testing. [C144-CH2]

### Work packages
1. What foulant species and fouling mechanisms are responsible for flux decline on the 10 kDa polyethersulfone membranes, measured against AFM surface characterisation and resistance-in-series analysis of membrane samples from production batches?
2. What pre-filtration configuration reduces cell-debris passage to the UF skid sufficiently to extend runs toward 72 hours, measured against flux-decline curves on a bench-scale crossflow rig using authentic fermentation broth?
3. What CIP protocol sequence and endpoint criteria restore water-flux recovery to baseline, measured against pure water flux recovery on both bench-scale membranes fouled under controlled conditions and authentic production membrane samples supplied by the company, tested before and after candidate CIP protocols?
4. Does the optimised combination of pre-filtration and CIP protocol sustain 72-hour runs at nominal flux on the production skid, measured against the two-year historical pressure and flow log baseline?
