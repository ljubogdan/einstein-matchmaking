# Urban LiDAR surveys delivered to clients in under 48 hours

**Company:** Geodata Pro d.o.o. [C126], Geospatial / GIS services, 34 employees, Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Anja Bošković, Research Associate, Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Civil Engineering and Geodesy [R0965].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Research associate (academic) and Technical lead / pipeline owner (company)  

### The problem
- A single city-block LiDAR flight produces raw point clouds in the range of 80 GB, and a full urban dataset takes between five and seven days end-to-end on the company's local cluster. [C126-CH1]
- Clients have started asking for delivery within 48 hours of the flight window closing, and the company needs either a faster automated pipeline or a cost model for burst cloud compute below a certain dataset size. [C126-CH1]
- Manual job-splitting across machines is in use but coordination overhead eats up the time saved, and one commercial cloud platform's per-job cost would exceed what most municipal contracts allow. [C126-CH1]

### Work packages
1. Can a tile-parallel GPU pipeline reduce end-to-end processing of urban LiDAR datasets up to 120 GB to under 48 hours, measured against the company's current five-to-seven-day baseline on twelve workstations?
2. What tile size and overlap width minimise boundary artefacts and inter-GPU communication overhead for datasets between 80 GB and 120 GB, measured against manually verified classified outputs on three representative city-block datasets?
3. At what dataset size threshold does on-premise GPU cluster processing become more cost-effective than burst cloud compute, measured against itemised cost logs for three dataset sizes run on each configuration?
4. Does automated ground classification and building extraction in the parallel pipeline match output quality of the existing sequential workflow, measured against three technicians' consensus labels on 2,000 sample points per dataset?
