# Finishing a stalled accounting data migration without manual surprises in each new batch

**Company:** Petrović & Lukić IT Preduzetnik [C124], Custom software and IT services for EU clients, 4 employees, Zrenjanin.  
**Researcher:** Slađana Ranković, Senior Research Associate, Faculty of Sciences, Department of Mathematics and Informatics [R0181].  
**Format:** Consulting  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Senior Research Associate, Department of Mathematics and Informatics (academic) and Managing director / lead developer (company)

### The problem
- The migration was quoted at 200 hours but is already at 340 hours and still not done, because every legacy export format is slightly different. [C124-CH2]
- Every annual backup from 2008 to 2016 uses a slightly different field structure, and two years of data were exported from an intermediate system that added its own non-standard columns. [C124-CH2]
- The client has a hard deadline of end of September because their auditor wants a full archive available before the annual review, and the company has no extra staff to assign. [C124-CH2]

### Work packages
1. Can structural analysis of the annual backup export files produce a complete taxonomy of format-variation patterns across the 2008–2016 legacy dataset?
2. Which pre-matching normalisation rules, when applied to the export files, reduce the rate at which newly encountered format quirks stall the migration pipeline?
3. Can a schema-drift detection mechanism flag previously unseen format variations before they require manual intervention?
4. How should the existing Python pipeline be extended to incorporate the recommended normalisation and matching steps without disrupting its current operation?

| Month 2 — Normalisation rules, matching approach, and drift detection | Design pre-matching normalisation rules, a schema-matching approach for the remaining batches, and a drift-detection step to flag novel variations, then document all three for pipeline integration. | A documented set of normalisation rules, a recommended schema-matching approach, and a drift-detection specification ready for implementation. |
