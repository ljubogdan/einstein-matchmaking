# Controlling humidity effects on corrugated box quality for pharmaceutical clients

**Company:** PakTeh d.o.o. [C138], Manufacturing – industrial packaging (corrugated board boxes, protective inserts, custom die-cut packaging), 28 employees, Subotica.  
**Researcher:** Đorđe Cvetković, Associate Professor, Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Graphic Engineering and Design [R0753].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Associate Professor, Department of Graphic Engineering and Design (academic) and Technical plant manager (company)

### The problem
- When humidity in the production hall goes above 65%, box compression strength drops by up to 18% and a pharma client has flagged two shipments for non-conformance this quarter. [C138-CH1]
- The hall has no humidity control system and no sensors distributed across it to provide real-time data. [C138-CH1]
- The owner is prepared to consider investment in a technical solution if a clear link between measurement and product quality can be demonstrated. [C138-CH1]

### Work packages
1. At which relative humidity thresholds and in which zones of the production hall does crease geometry on E-flute board processed by the Rotermat DC-420 fall outside acceptable limits?
2. What is the quantitative relationship between zone-specific humidity readings and BCT loss for E-flute 1.2–1.4 mm board at this facility?
3. How do matrix wear and ambient humidity interact over a production shift, and what matrix replacement interval does that interaction suggest during high-humidity periods?
4. What multi-point sensor placement and alert thresholds constitute a practical, low-cost protocol for predicting non-conformance events before boxes leave the cutting line?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Sensor deployment and baseline mapping (months 1–2) | Install multi-point humidity and temperature loggers at agreed positions across the production hall, covering board storage, die-cutting, and dispatch zones; collect continuous baseline data alongside existing BCT records. | Hall humidity profiles across all monitored zones established, with baseline dataset ready for threshold analysis (M1). |
| Controlled production trials and threshold analysis (months 3–5) | Run controlled test batches on the Rotermat DC-420 under naturally occurring and chamber-validated humidity conditions; measure crease channel depth, shoulder symmetry, and BCT at each condition; correlate zone-specific sensor readings with quality outcomes. | Humidity threshold report identifying critical hall zones and conditions causing crease geometry degradation and BCT loss on E-flute 1.2–1.4 mm board delivered (M2). |
| Protocol development and BCT validation (months 6–8) | Design a practical humidity monitoring and prediction protocol based on identified thresholds; validate it against production BCT measurements across multiple production days; refine sensor placement and alert logic. | Validated humidity control and prediction protocol with full BCT correlation dataset delivered (M3). |
| Final specification and reporting (month 9) | Compile all measurement data, threshold findings, and validation results into a final technical report; formulate a humidity management specification covering sensor positions, alert thresholds, and recommended intervention actions. | Final report and recommended humidity management specification for the facility delivered (M4). |

**University** [R0753]
- Field measurements at corrugated board converting plants in the Pannonian region show that each 10-percentage-point increase in storage relative humidity is associated with a 4.2 kN decline in mean BCT. [SYN-PUB-08306]
- Continuous monitoring of a rotary die-cutter shows that relative humidity accounts for 38% of the variance in crease channel depth and 44% of the variance in shoulder asymmetry, with shoulder asymmetry increasing markedly when humidity exceeds 68%. [SYN-PUB-08310]
- Multi-point humidity logging at converting plants in the Pannonian region shows mean daily relative humidity in board storage ranging from 34% in winter to 71% in summer with peaks reaching 82%. [SYN-PUB-08306]
