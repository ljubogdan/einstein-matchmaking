# Knowing which hall zones and humidity levels break E-flute quality

**Company:** PakTeh d.o.o. [C138], Manufacturing – industrial packaging (corrugated board boxes, protective inserts, custom die-cut packaging), 28 employees, Subotica.  
**Researcher:** Đorđe Cvetković, Associate Professor, Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Graphic Engineering and Design [R0753].  
**Format:** Consulting  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Associate Professor, Department of Graphic Engineering and Design (academic) and Technical manager (production) (company)  

### The problem
- The Rotermat DC-420 produces burrs and uneven creases on E-flute material when hall humidity exceeds 65%, and box compression strength drops between 14 and 18 percent compared with dry days. [C138-CH1]
- A pharma client returned non-conformance documentation because boxes did not meet the requirement of 3,2 kN BCT. [C138-CH1]
- The facility has no humidity control system and no sensors distributed across hall zones to provide real-time data. [C138-CH1]

### Work packages
1. At which relative humidity thresholds does crease shoulder asymmetry on E-flute blanks from the Rotermat DC-420 exceed the tolerance accepted by the pharma client, measured against caliper and profilometer readings on 200 blanks per humidity level?
2. Which hall zones show the strongest correlation between local RH and BCT loss on finished boxes, measured against the company's internal BCT records for the same production batches?
3. Does a set of low-cost fixed-point humidity sensors placed in the identified critical zones predict BCT non-conformance events, measured against ISO 12048 BCT measurements taken on sampled lots from the same production runs?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 – Sensor deployment and baseline logging (months 1–2) | Researcher designs a multi-point logging layout; company technical manager installs low-cost RH sensors (purchased within the agreed budget) at storage, die-cutting, and dispatch zones; sensors log at 5-minute intervals. | A six-week continuous humidity and temperature dataset covering all hall zones, plus matched BCT and crease-quality records from the company's existing logs. |
| Phase 2 – Threshold and zone analysis (months 2–4) | Researcher analyses logged RH data against BCT records and crease measurements taken by the technical manager on sampled blanks; scripted humidity excursions are documented using the climate chamber at the laboratory for bench-scale validation. | A written threshold analysis report identifying the RH levels and hall zones correlated with BCT drop and crease defects, with supporting statistical tables. |
| Phase 3 – Sensor validation and monitoring specification (months 4–5) | Technical manager collects sampled box lots during production runs spanning low and high RH events; researcher measures BCT on each sampled lot by ISO 12048 and compares results against sensor readings to validate the prediction logic; researcher then drafts a sensor placement and alert specification. | A validation dataset pairing sensor RH readings with ISO 12048 BCT values, plus a monitoring specification with sensor positions, threshold values, alert rules, and an itemised hardware cost estimate within the 15,000 EUR budget. |
| Phase 4 – Final report and implementation checklist (month 6) | Researcher consolidates findings; company technical manager reviews for confidential process details; final report is delivered after the 30-day review window. | A final report with the recommended low-cost solution, an implementation checklist, and a one-page operator guide the production team can use on the floor. |
