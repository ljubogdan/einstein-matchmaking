# Keeping transaction response times under 200 ms as daily volume grows to 500,000

**Company:** Voltis Pay d.o.o. [C143], Fintech, 47 employees, Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Strahinja Radović, Research Associate, Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Computing and Control Engineering [R0385].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Research Associate as principal investigator (academic) and CTO or designated senior engineer as technical liaison (company)  

### The problem
- The PostgreSQL cluster records latency of 320 to 450 ms during evening peaks, breaching the 200 ms SLA the company has committed to clients. [C143-CH2]
- The most expensive operations are complex JOIN queries across transaction, user-profile, and partner-configuration tables, and the company is uncertain which of caching or read distribution on replicas yields the greater gain. [C143-CH2]

### Work packages
1. Which combination of read-distribution policy and query routing rules reduces peak-hour tail latency to below 200 ms, measured against Grafana traces from 30 consecutive evening peak windows?
2. Does a composite partitioning scheme on the transactions table lower 95th-percentile JOIN latency more than a caching layer alone, measured against the same 30-window baseline?
3. At what daily transaction volume does the recommended routing strategy sustain the 200 ms bound without further structural change, measured against synthetic load injected in the sandbox up to 500,000 transactions per day?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 — Baseline diagnosis (months 1–2) | Researcher analyses Grafana and Prometheus traces and query logs provided by the engineering team; engineering team exports anonymised sandbox data and annotates peak-window incidents. | Written diagnosis report identifying root causes of peak latency, delivered at M2. |
| Phase 2 — Strategy design and sandbox validation (months 3–5) | Researcher designs query routing and read-distribution strategy drawing on latency-bounded scheduling and partitioning methods; engineering team deploys candidate configuration in sandbox and runs load tests up to 500,000 transactions per day. | Prototype routing and read-distribution configuration validated in sandbox, with measured latency results against the 30-window baseline, delivered at M5. |
| Phase 3 — Tuning and benchmarking (months 6–8) | Researcher refines strategy based on sandbox results; engineering team runs scripted peak-load scenarios in sandbox and records 95th-percentile and median latency at each transaction-volume step. | Tuned solution with full performance benchmark dataset showing latency at load levels from 180,000 to 500,000 daily transactions, delivered at M8. |
| Phase 4 — Handover (month 9) | Researcher prepares handover documentation; engineering team and researcher conduct a knowledge-transfer session covering configuration rationale and operating procedures. | Handover document and session notes enabling the engineering team to operate and extend the solution independently, delivered at M9. |
