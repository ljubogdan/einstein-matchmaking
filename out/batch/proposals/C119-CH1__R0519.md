# Faster artwork-to-print across packaging dieline variants

**Company:** Vizura Studio d.o.o. [C119], Marketing tech, media and creative studios, 14 employees, Novi Sad.  
**Researcher:** Rastko Malešević, Assistant Professor, Faculty of Technical Sciences, Department of Graphic Engineering and Design [R0519].  
**Format:** Contract research  
**Supervisors / contacts:** Assistant Professor, Department of Graphic Engineering and Design (academic) and Project coordinator (Vizura Studio) (company)

### The problem
- The studio produces roughly 600 packaging variants a year and the bottleneck is taking finished artwork and fitting it into the structural dieline for that specific SKU. [C119-CH1]
- On a shaped carton or a multi-panel sleeve the manual step can take two full days, making the average packaging turnaround five days from approved artwork to print-ready file. [C119-CH1]
- Scripting of repetitive placement steps was attempted but the dielines vary too much in anchor points and bleed zones for a simple macro to handle reliably. [C119-CH1]

### Work packages
1. How can structural dieline geometry be parsed to automatically identify printable panels, anchor points, and bleed zones across rectangular and shaped carton formats?
2. What constraint-driven rules enable reliable placement and scaling of approved artwork across dieline geometries that differ in panel count and shape?
3. How much designer review time remains necessary after automated placement to reach a print-ready file that meets offset and digital vendor requirements?
4. How can the placement engine be embedded in an Illustrator and Esko ArtPro workflow to produce vendor-compatible output without additional file conversion?

### Plan
| Phase | Work | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 – Dieline parser (months 1–3) | Develop a dieline geometry parser using studio-supplied dieline samples covering rectangular and simple shaped carton formats; map printable panels, anchor points, and bleed zones for each tested type. | Parser prototype validated on rectangular and simple shaped carton formats, with documented panel-identification accuracy, addressing the research question on dieline geometry parsing. |
| Phase 2 – Placement engine (months 4–6) | Design and implement constraint-driven rules for artwork placement and scaling; validate against a set of studio dieline variants and record cases requiring designer correction to measure residual review time. | Placement engine with accuracy metrics and a documented catalogue of correction cases, addressing the research questions on placement rules and remaining designer review time. |
| Phase 3 – Workflow integration (months 7–9) | Integrate the placement engine as an extension operable alongside Adobe Illustrator and Esko ArtPro; run designer review sessions with studio staff to refine the interface and confirm file format compatibility. | Integration prototype ready for vendor file format testing, with notes on any remaining manual steps, addressing the research question on workflow embedding. |
| Phase 4 – Vendor validation and delivery (months 10–12) | Test print-ready output with offset and digital print vendors; resolve any file format issues; complete project report and deliver all results to the studio. | Print-ready file output validated with offset and digital print vendors, and final project report delivered to the studio. |
